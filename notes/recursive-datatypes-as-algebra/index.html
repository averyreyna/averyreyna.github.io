Recursive Datatypes as Algebra

A binary tree is T = 1 + T × T: a leaf is the unit/INL side, and a node is a pair of subtrees on the INR side. This equation is an isomorphism, not a literal unfolding-forever definition, and the subtlety is worth sitting with.

fold and unfold

Two functions witness the isomorphism. unfold is case/pattern match: Leaf goes to (), and Node(l, r) goes to the pair (l, r). fold runs the constructors: () goes to Leaf, and (l, r) goes to Node(l, r).

They are mutually inverse, so round-tripping any tree through unfold then fold returns the exact same tree. That round-trip property, checkable on real values, is the whole content of "isomorphism." Nothing deeper is going on.

HW1: Reading Off the Algebra

Three SML datatypes, each read straight into its algebraic equation:

  • datatype r = A | B of r | C of (r*r*r) is r = 1 + r + r³
  • datatype t = Z | S of t is t = 1 + t, the naturals in Peano encoding
  • datatype 'a u = NN | SS of 'a is u = 1 + a. It is not recursive, since u does not appear on the right, so there is nothing to solve, just a description. This is Option/Maybe under different constructor names.

Notes

Because T = 1 + T² is a genuine isomorphism, it is tempting to treat T as an algebraic unknown and solve it like a real equation. That temptation is where things go wrong, and also, strangely, somewhere true.
