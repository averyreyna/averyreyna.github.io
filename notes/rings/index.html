Rings

A ring is a set with two operations (+ and ·) where (R, +) is an abelian group and multiplication is associative and distributes over addition. The integers ℤ are the canonical example.

  • A commutative ring has commutative multiplication, and most rings you'll meet are commutative
  • A field is a commutative ring where every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse (ℚ, ℝ, ℂ)
  • Ideals are the "normal subgroups" of rings, used to construct quotient rings
  • Rings are objects in the category Ring, relevant when studying universal constructions

Aluffi's Algebra: Chapter 0 introduces groups and rings entirely through categorical language, worth reading alongside category theory rather than before it.
