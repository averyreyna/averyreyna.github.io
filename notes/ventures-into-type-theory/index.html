Ventures into Type Theory

A theory-of-programming-languages week on algebraic data types. Products and sums turn out to be an AND/OR duality: unconditional in both directions for products, conditional in both for sums, which is why case/match has to branch and be exhaustive. Recursive types like binary trees are an honest isomorphism between the constructors and a set-theoretic equation, witnessed by fold and unfold, and that equation invites a tempting but illegitimate move: solve T=1+T² for T as if it were a complex number and land on a real result anyway (Seven Trees in One), then find the legitimate version of the same trick in generating functions, which count trees by size instead of solving for T directly. The legitimate half keeps paying off: differentiating a datatype's equation produces its zipper, with the derivative's coefficients falling out as real one-hole-context choices.
