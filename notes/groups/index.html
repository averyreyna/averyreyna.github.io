Groups

A group is a set G with a binary operation · satisfying closure, associativity, identity, and inverses. The integers under addition form a group, and so do the nonzero rationals under multiplication.

  • A subgroup H ⊆ G is a subset closed under the group operation and inverses
  • A homomorphism is a map f : G → H that preserves structure: f(a · b) = f(a) · f(b)
  • The kernel of a homomorphism is itself a normal subgroup
  • Groups appear throughout category theory as objects in the category Grp, alongside sets in Set and topological spaces in Top

The symmetries of any object form a group. This is why group theory is so pervasive in math and physics.

Homomorphisms, not groups themselves, are the real subject: they are exactly the structure-preserving morphisms that functors generalize one level up.
