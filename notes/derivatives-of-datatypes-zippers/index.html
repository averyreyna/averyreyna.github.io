Derivatives of Datatypes: Zippers

A zipper gives O(1) local navigation via a cursor instead of O(n) re-traversal: the data at the cursor, plus a "context" describing how to rebuild everything around it. Differentiating a datatype's algebraic equation produces exactly this context, and the calculus turns out to be legitimate the whole way through, unlike solving the equation itself.

List Zipper

A list is L(α) = 1 + α · L(α). Differentiating gives L'(1−α) = L, so L'(α) = L(α)², literally a pair of lists: before-cursor and after-cursor. That matches the informal zipper idea exactly.

Tree Zipper and the "2 = bool" Insight

A tree with values at nodes is T(α) = 1 + α · T(α)². Differentiating with the product and chain rules:

T'(α) = T(α)² + 2α · T(α) · T'(α)

T'(α) = T(α)² / (1 − 2α · T(α)) = T(α)² × L(2α · T(α))

Reading the result: T(α)² is the current node's two children, needed to descend back down. L(2 · T(α)) is the ancestor chain back to the root, a list of steps, each step a direction (left or right, a bool, i.e. the type 2) plus the sibling subtree not taken.

The 2 is not a scalar. It falls out of the product rule on T × T (d(T × T) = T'T + TT' = 2TT'), and combinatorially it answers "is the hole in the left T or the right T?", a genuine two-way choice. The calculus's power-rule coefficient and an actual data field you would have to store both mean the same thing here. Every calculus rule (sum, product, chain, power) corresponds to a real, checkable operation on "where could the hole be."

HW2: One-Hole Context for Binode/Trinode

The same trick on a tree with two- and three-way branching:

datatype 'a t = Empty
              | Binode of 'a * ('a t * 'a t)
              | Trinode of 'a * ('a t * 'a t * 'a t)

The algebra is T = 1 + a·T² + a·T³. Differentiating:

T' = (T²+T³) + a · (2T+3T²) · T' = (T²+T³) × L(2aT+3aT²)

T²+T³ is the case where the search stopped here, meaning this node's own payload is the hole and its children are untouched. L(2aT+3aT²) is a list of steps down, each either a Binode step (2 choices of which child) or a Trinode step (3 choices):

(* one step on the way down *)
datatype 'a step =
    BinodeL of 'a * 'a t
  | BinodeR of 'a * 'a t
  | Trinode1 of 'a * 'a t * 'a t
  | Trinode2 of 'a * 'a t * 'a t
  | Trinode3 of 'a * 'a t * 'a t

(* where the search stops *)
datatype 'a innermost =
    HereBinode  of 'a t * 'a t
  | HereTrinode of 'a t * 'a t * 'a t

(* the full one-hole context *)
type 'a context = 'a step list * 'a innermost

A sanity check in the fold style: filling the hole and walking the step list back up should exactly reconstruct the original tree, the same round-trip property as fold/unfold.

fun fillInnermost (h: 'a) (HereBinode (l, r))     = Binode (h, (l, r))
  | fillInnermost (h: 'a) (HereTrinode (x, y, z)) = Trinode (h, (x, y, z))

fun fill ((steps, inner): 'a context, h: 'a) : 'a t =
  let
    fun rebuild [] node = node
      | rebuild (BinodeL (p, r) :: rest)     node = rebuild rest (Binode  (p, (node, r)))
      | rebuild (BinodeR (p, l) :: rest)     node = rebuild rest (Binode  (p, (l, node)))
      | rebuild (Trinode1 (p, y, z) :: rest) node = rebuild rest (Trinode (p, (node, y, z)))
      | rebuild (Trinode2 (p, x, z) :: rest) node = rebuild rest (Trinode (p, (x, node, z)))
      | rebuild (Trinode3 (p, x, y) :: rest) node = rebuild rest (Trinode (p, (x, y, node)))
  in
    rebuild steps (fillInnermost h inner)
  end
