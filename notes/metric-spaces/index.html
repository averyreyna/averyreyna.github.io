Metric Spaces

A metric space is a set X with a distance function d : X × X → ℝ satisfying non-negativity, identity of indiscernibles, symmetry, and the triangle inequality.

  • Every metric space is automatically a topological space, since the open balls form a basis
  • Cauchy sequences and completeness: a metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence converges (ℝ is complete, ℚ is not)
  • Uniform continuity is stronger than continuity: the ε can be chosen independently of the point
  • Metric spaces appear in the UH course sequence (MATH 4337/6342) as the concrete entry point into topology

Metric spaces are the comfortable middle ground between analysis and topology. Most examples in category theory that involve topology start as metric spaces before being generalized.
