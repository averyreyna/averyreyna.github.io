Domain Theory

Domain theory provides a mathematical foundation for denotational semantics, assigning mathematical meanings to programs. A domain is a particular kind of complete partial order (CPO): a set with a least element ⊥ and joins for directed subsets, where ⊥ represents "undefined" or nontermination.

  • Scott continuity: a function between domains is continuous if it preserves directed joins, generalizing topological continuity
  • Recursive functions are interpreted as least fixed points of monotone endofunctions on a domain (Kleene's fixed-point theorem)
  • Domain theory connects lattice theory (CPOs are specialized lattices) to the Scott topology, which lives in topology

Domain theory is why denotational semantics can give meanings to recursive programs: a while loop is the least fixed point of a monotone function on a domain of partial functions.
