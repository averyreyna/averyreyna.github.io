Topological Spaces

A topological space is a set X with a collection of "open sets" satisfying: ∅ and X are open, arbitrary unions of open sets are open, and finite intersections of open sets are open. Open sets capture the idea of "nearness" without requiring a precise notion of distance.

  • A basis for a topology is a collection of sets from which all open sets can be built by unions
  • The discrete topology on X treats every subset as open (the finest topology)
  • The indiscrete topology has only ∅ and X as open sets (the coarsest topology)
  • Topological spaces are objects in the category Top, a core running example in category theory alongside Set and Grp

Topology is where category theory's examples live: functors, natural transformations, and adjoints all have natural instantiations in Top.
