Moving Through Abstraction

I mean to write a proper blog post about this but I think we have the AI-assisted programming conversation all wrong. Writing code by hand and LLM-driven development don't necessarily have to stand as opposing forces. Good, proper software fundamentals are, and always will be, important as an engineer. I think it's misplaced to call vibe coding pure dopamine hits. Do some people just prompt for what they want and don't bother learning code or industry standard practices that make life easier? Yeah. However, code has always been an interface that requires deep technical knowledge (or whatever depth is required for the proper maintenance of software systems). Now that interface is malleable.

Data Hoarding

Currently reading about shadow libraries, and by extension, pirate archivists. First started reading Anna's Blog to get acquainted with the subject.

I somehow philosophically relate to the mission of these pirates; I often (non-conspiratorially) think about who controls digital media and what that means for cultural preservation.

It's funny to read about this a couple years out of college since I used my fair share of shadow libraries to get textbooks for those 3000-level classes that require insider editions for reading materials.

The inclusion of these repositories in the datasets of LLMs, especially at the early stages ~three years ago, is sorta paradoxical. The Common Crawl dataset has been tokenized by GenAI applications for what seems like forever. The (un-)paywalled content is a part of culture.

I mean... fundamentally this phenomenon is a natural response to the privatization of academia's pools of knowledge. Of course it's not all bad: the ACM Digital Library is now open access and having complete access to the conference proceedings of HCI venues, for instance, is on net a good thing.

Part of the reason why AI is so hated is because of the black box of it all. Do you think people really want a tech company promising the automation of thought? Do you think the average person can explain what "AI" really encapsulates? It's become a misnomer fueled by societal projections, sometimes rightfully so.

The cultural metabolism for new technology is fast-tracked if science communication is easy to understand and starts from a place of familiarity, likely rooted in history as context.
