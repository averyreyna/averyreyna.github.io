CRDT Introduction

Conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs) are crucial for collaborative apps. Users expect to see their own operations immediately, without waiting for a round-trip to a central server, especially in a local-first app, where edits happen even while offline. Each device therefore applies operations to its own replica and the replicas must converge later.

Two Kinds

Look up the definition and you will find two main kinds, defined with mathematical vocabulary that is probably already more complicated than your mental model of a collaborative app:

  • Op-based CRDTs broadcast each operation to every replica; convergence requires concurrent operations to commute, so applying them in either order gives the same state
  • State-based CRDTs exchange whole states and merge them; the states form a join-semilattice and merge is the least upper bound (idempotent, commutative, and associative by construction)

Notes

The framing I want to keep from Weidner's survey: start from what collaborative apps actually need (immediate local application, eventual agreement) and let the algebra be the implementation detail, not the definition.

Source: Matt Weidner's CRDT survey, part 1.
